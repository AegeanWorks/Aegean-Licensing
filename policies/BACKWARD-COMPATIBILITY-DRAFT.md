# Aegean Licensing — Backward Compatibility Policy (DRAFT)

**Status:** Draft for review; not an operative license or a retroactive amendment to any prior license.

## Purpose

Aegean Licensing aims to keep licensing consistent across current releases and older versions that are newly distributed from an official Aegean source, while preserving valid rights already granted to recipients.

## Policy

1. **Explicit license per release.** Every official release must identify the exact license name and version that applies to that release.
2. **Current terms for new releases.** After the Aegean License 1.0 is finalized, new releases will use the license identified in the release notice.
3. **Older versions offered again.** The rights holder may reissue an older version under the current Aegean license only if it owns or has obtained permission to license all relevant material under those terms. The download page and archive must explicitly say that the copy is offered under the current license.
4. **No retroactive cancellation.** A new license does not cancel or narrow a valid license already granted for an existing copy. Recipients of copies already released under AGPL-3.0 or another license may continue to rely on that license according to its terms.
5. **Third-party material.** Dependencies, assets, and contributions remain governed by their own licenses unless the rights holder has the legal authority and permission needed to change those terms.
6. **Forks and mirrors.** Aegean Licensing cannot use this policy to compel independent fork owners, mirrors, or prior recipients to replace a previously valid license with the current one.
7. **Document changes.** Changes to licensing policy must be recorded in `CHANGELOG.md` and in the release notes for affected projects.

## Important limit

“Older versions are subject to current licensing” can apply to older copies newly reissued by the rights holder under the current license, where the necessary rights are controlled. It cannot universally override permissions previously granted under AGPL-3.0 or any other valid prior license.

For a project such as Chronos that has already been released under AGPL-3.0, a transition plan must preserve the existing AGPL grants and assess contributions and dependencies before any version is reissued under different terms.
